\section*{Why this document exists}

The manuscript cited 27 works whose bibliographic metadata had been resolved mechanically
(CrossRef, OpenAlex, DataCite). Metadata verification proves a paper \emph{exists} and is
correctly attributed. It does not prove the author has \emph{read} it, and several sentences in
\textsection II make claims about what those papers found. This audit closes that gap: every entry
is classified by how much of it was actually obtained and examined, and each manuscript claim is
checked against the source text.

\section*{Result in one line}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.93\textwidth}{\centering
Of 27 cited works, \textbf{19 were obtainable} and \textbf{8 are paywalled}.
The passages actually read: \textbf{16} full-text or abstract, \textbf{3} partial.
\textbf{2 citation errors} were found and corrected; \textbf{1 manuscript claim} was found
unsupported and softened. \textbf{8 works rest on metadata alone} and are listed at \S4.}}
\end{center}

\section{Corrections made as a result of reading}

\subsection*{C1 --- \texttt{zou2023universal}: two authors were missing \fix}

The bibliography listed four authors. The paper has \textbf{six}:
\begin{quote}\small
Andy Zou, Zifan Wang, \textbf{Nicholas Carlini}, \textbf{Milad Nasr}, J. Zico Kolter,
Matt Fredrikson.
\end{quote}
Carlini and Nasr were absent. This is the kind of error a reviewer finds by clicking the arXiv
link, so it matters more than its size. Corrected to the full six-author list.

\subsection*{C2 --- \texttt{yang2024falsepositives}: two authors did not exist \fix}

The bibliography listed seven authors, including \emph{Jin, Yidan} and \emph{Chen, Zhenyu}. Neither
appears on the paper. The authoritative list is five:
\begin{quote}\small
Yixin Yang, Ming Wen, Xiang Gao, Yuting Zhang, \textbf{Hailong Sun}.
\end{quote}
Two names had been carried over from a search result rather than from the record of the paper
itself, and a real author (Sun) was missing. Corrected to five.

\subsection*{C3 --- \texttt{hou2024llmse}: our claim was broader than the paper \weak}

The manuscript used this citation to support: \emph{``the transition from LLMs as text generators
to LLMs as actors is the defining architectural shift in contemporary software engineering.''}

The paper is a systematic literature review of LLMs for software engineering across code
generation, testing, and maintenance. It surveys \emph{LLMs in SE generally}, and is not a study
of the agent-as-actor shift, nor does it make that claim. Our sentence was attributing to it an
argument it does not make. Softened to cite it for the area being surveyed, not the thesis.

\section{Still unread, and what to obtain}

Eight works rest on verified metadata alone. Four matter to an argument in the manuscript; four
are cited as context and are lower risk.

\subsection*{Would change the manuscript if read}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{zhao2025 --- Soleker}, IEEE/ACM ASE 2025, DOI \texttt{10.1109/ase63991.2025.00209}.
  \emph{The highest-value unread paper.} It is the closest related work: Solana vulnerability
  detection. Its abstract establishes that it analyses \emph{contract bytecode}, which is what
  makes our client-layer gap a real gap. If the full text happens to cover Python client tooling,
  our novelty claim would need revisiting --- the abstract suggests it does not, but I have not
  confirmed that. \emph{Xplore document id 11334576.}
\item \textbf{yang2024fp --- Reducing False Positives of Static Bug Detectors}, SANER 2024,
  DOI \texttt{10.1109/saner60148.2024.00075}. We invoke it as precedent for measuring precision on
  real code. Worth confirming it says what the title implies.
\item \textbf{kuhne2022 --- Multi-dimensional multi-level modeling}, SoSyM 2022,
  DOI \texttt{10.1007/s10270-021-00951-5}. We cite it for a deliberately narrow point, so the risk
  is low, but it is the citation that anchors the doctoral direction.
\item \textbf{ulain2024 --- } the CIM 2024 interpretable-feature-vectors paper would be the
  stronger citation; it is paywalled at Xplore, and her free TechRxiv preprint returns 403 to
  automated fetching. A manual download should work.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Lower risk (cited as context)}

\texttt{cao2026survey}, \texttt{borlakova2026}, \texttt{liu2025}, \texttt{yang2024sokai},
\texttt{gandhi2026}. Each is used to establish that a field exists or is active, not to support a
specific finding. Metadata verification is proportionate here, though reading them would still be
better.

\section*{Bottom line}

The bibliography is now accurate in its \emph{facts} --- two author-list errors were real and are
fixed, and one claim was over-broad and is softened. What remains is coverage: nineteen works were
reachable and sixteen were examined, but the single most consequential related-work paper for this
manuscript's novelty argument (Soleker) has been read only at abstract level. That is stated here
rather than left implicit, and the paper's \textsection II does not depend on it beyond the claim
its abstract already supports.
